Antiviral Agents & Viral Infections

Because viruses are obligate intracellular parasites integrated into host cell machinery, achieving selective toxicity requires targeting viral-specific enzymes (e.g., viral thymidine kinase, reverse transcriptase, protease, integrase). Therapy must be initiated early during active viral replication for optimal efficacy.

Aciclovir

Nucleoside Analogue

Phosphorylated by viral-specific thymidine kinase to monophosphate, then by host kinases to active triphosphate. Competes as viral DNA polymerase substrate & acts as obligate chain terminator.

  • Bioavailability: ~20% oral
  • Half-Life ($t_{1/2}$): ~3 hours
  • Distribution: CSF ~50% of plasma; low in brain (high IV doses required for viral encephalitis)
  • Excretion: Predominantly renal (dose adjustment required)
  • Dosing: 5x/day (acute) or 2x/day (suppressive)
⚠️ Key Warning: Rapid IV infusion can cause crystal-induced acute renal failure. Prevent with slow infusion and robust hydration.

Valaciclovir

Prodrug

L-valine ester prodrug of aciclovir. Enzymatically hydrolyzed in the liver to aciclovir post-absorption.

  • Bioavailability: ~60% oral (3x higher than aciclovir)
  • Dosing Frequency: 8-hourly (more convenient regimen)
  • Indications: Herpes zoster, mucocutaneous HSV, prevention of post-transplant CMV transmission.

Famciclovir / Penciclovir

Prodrug / Topical

Famciclovir is a diacetyl ester prodrug of penciclovir ($t_{1/2} \approx 2\text{ h}$). Dosed 8-hourly orally for herpes zoster and genital/labial HSV.

Penciclovir: Available as a topical cream for recurrent herpes labialis.
Idoxuridine / Trifluridine: Older topical nucleoside analogues superseded by aciclovir due to ocular irritation and systemic toxicity.

HIV ART Principles & Goals

Goal: Maximal viral suppression (< 50 copies/mL) to restore CD4 counts and prevent transmission (U=U). Immediate initiation upon diagnosis. Urgent if CD4 < 200 cells/µL, AIDS-defining illness, HBV co-infection, or pregnancy. Combination HAART prevents single-mutation resistance.

1. NRTIs (Nucleoside/Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors)

Competitive inhibitors integrated into viral DNA causing premature chain termination. Require host intracellular phosphorylation.

Abacavir (ABC): $t_{1/2} \approx 2\text{ h}$.
⚠️ Mandatory screening for HLA-B*5701 allele (prevent severe hypersensitivity reaction).
Tenofovir (TDF / TAF): Nucleotide ($t_{1/2} \approx 17\text{ h}$). Active vs HIV & HBV. TAF has higher intracellular levels with lower renal/bone toxicity than TDF (Fanconi syndrome risk).
Zidovudine (AZT): Bioavailability ~60%, CSF ~50%. Causes severe bone marrow suppression (anaemia, neutropenia) and lactic acidosis. Do not combine with Stavudine.
Lamivudine (3TC) & Emtricitabine (FTC): Intracellular $t_{1/2} \approx 14\text{ h}$. High safety profile. Active vs HBV (high HBV resistance if used long-term). Do not combine 3TC + FTC.

2. NNRTIs (Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors)

Non-competitive inhibitors binding hydrophobic pocket near RT active site. HIV-1 specific. Metabolized by CYP450.

Efavirenz: $t_{1/2} \approx 52\text{ h}$ (once daily). CNS side effects in 50% (vivid dreams, depression; dose at bedtime). Teratogenic. Mutation K103N confers cross-resistance.
Nevirapine: $t_{1/2} \approx 28\text{ h}$. Auto-induces CYP metabolism (2-week lead-in required). Severe Stevens-Johnson syndrome & fatal hepatitis risk.
Rilpivirine & Etravirine: Rilpivirine requires acidic pH/food ($t_{1/2} \approx 50\text{ h}$). Etravirine active vs NNRTI-resistant strains.

3. Protease Inhibitors (PIs)

Prevent cleavage of gag-pol precursor polyproteins into functional viral enzymes.

Agents: Atazanavir, Darunavir, Lopinavir, Ritonavir, Saquinavir.

Adverse Effects: Dyslipidaemia, insulin resistance, lipodystrophy, GI distress.

PK Boosting: Low-dose Ritonavir or Cobicistat inhibits CYP3A4, dramatically elevating co-administered PI plasma concentrations.

Specific Toxicities: Atazanavir (asymptomatic hyperbilirubinaemia); Indinavir (nephrolithiasis/kidney stones).

4. INSTIs & Fusion/Entry Inhibitors

Integrase Inhibitors (INSTIs): Block integration of viral DNA.
  • Dolutegravir: High genetic barrier to resistance. Must NOT be given with polyvalent cations (Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, Fe²⁺ antacids).
  • Raltegravir: Metabolized via glucuronidation. Risk of rhabdomyolysis.
Entry & Fusion Inhibitors:
  • Enfuvirtide: 36-amino acid peptide targeting gp41 fusion ($t_{1/2} \approx 4\text{ h}$ SC).
  • Maraviroc: CCR5 co-receptor antagonist. Requires Trofile assay to rule out CXCR4-tropic virus.

Oseltamivir

Oral Neuraminidase Inhibitor

Inhibits viral neuraminidase, preventing viral release from infected cells. Reduces symptom duration if given within 36h of onset.

Resistance: His274Tyr mutation in seasonal H1N1.

Zanamivir

Inhaled Dry Powder

Low bioavailability (~2%). Preferred in pregnancy due to minimal systemic exposure.

Warning: Risk of severe bronchospasm in asthmatics/COPD.

Peramivir & Amantadine

IV Neuraminidase / M2 Blocker

Peramivir: Single IV dose reserved for severe hospital-acquired cases.

Amantadine: Targets M2 uncoating protein. Widespread resistance; no longer recommended for Influenza A.

Ganciclovir & Valganciclovir

Guanosine analogues inhibiting viral DNA polymerase. Used for sight- or life-threatening CMV in immunocompromised hosts.

Valganciclovir: Oral prodrug with high bioavailability.

Major Toxicity: Marked myelosuppression (profound neutropenia, thrombocytopenia).

Foscarnet & Cidofovir

Alternative direct DNA polymerase inhibitors for ganciclovir-resistant CMV or aciclovir-resistant HSV.

Foscarnet: Pyrophosphate analogue. Highly nephrotoxic; causes severe hypocalcaemia and genital ulcers.

Cidofovir: Nucleotide analogue. Administered IV with co-infused probenecid and saline hydration to reduce renal injury.

Ribavirin

Synthetic nucleoside aerosolized for severe infant RSV bronchiolitis; oral form ($t_{1/2} \approx 45\text{ h}$) used for Hepatitis C & Lassa fever.

Teratogenic & causes severe haemolytic anaemia.

Palivizumab

Humanized monoclonal antibody targeting RSV F-glycoprotein.

Given IM monthly as immunoprophylaxis in high-risk premature infants.

Pegylated Interferon-α

Enhances viral RNA degradation. Used in chronic Hepatitis B/C and Kaposi sarcoma.

Side effects: Flu-like syndrome, severe depression, bone marrow suppression.

Antifungal Therapeutics & Mycoses

Fungal cells possess eukaryotic cell structures but feature unique fungal cell walls ($\beta\text{-(1,3)-D-glucan}$) and fungal cell membranes rich in ergosterol rather than cholesterol. Therapeutic strategy focuses on selectively disrupting membrane stability, cell wall synthesis, or nuclear division.

Interactive Fungal Site of Action Map

1. Cell Wall Glucan Synthesis
Echinocandins

Inhibit $\beta$-(1,3)-D-glucan synthase (Caspofungin, Micafungin, Anidulafungin).

2. Ergosterol Membrane Pores
Polyenes

Bind directly to ergosterol creating membrane ion pores (Amphotericin B, Nystatin).

3. Ergosterol Synthesis
Azoles & Allylamines

Inhibit $14\alpha$-demethylase (Azoles) or squalene epoxidase (Terbinafine).

4. DNA / Mitosis Inhibitors
Flucytosine & Griseofulvin

Block RNA/DNA synthesis (5-FC) or fungal tubulin polymerization (Griseofulvin).

Polyenes

Amphotericin B:
  • Pharmacokinetics: Minimal oral absorption (IV only). $t_{1/2} \approx 15\text{ days}$.
  • Dosing: 0.7–1 mg/kg standard; 3–10 mg/kg lipid formulations (AmBisome). Lipid complexes significantly reduce nephrotoxicity.
  • Adverse Effects: Distal RTA leading to severe hypokalaemia & hypomagnesaemia; fevers, chills, anaemia.
Nystatin: Highly toxic systemically; strictly used topically or orally for non-invasive mucocutaneous candidiasis.

Triazoles & Imidazoles

Fluconazole ($t_{1/2} \approx 30\text{ h}$): High CSF penetration, 80% renal excretion unchanged. Choice agent for cryptococcal meningitis & mucosal candidiasis.
Voriconazole ($t_{1/2} \approx 7\text{ h}$): First-line for invasive Aspergillus. Unique toxicity: transient visual disturbances (visual blurring, color alteration in 30%) and photosensitivity.
Itraconazole ($t_{1/2} \approx 25\text{--}40\text{ h}$): Requires acidic pH for absorption. Potent CYP3A4 inhibitor. Risk of congestive heart failure.

Echinocandins & Terbinafine

Echinocandins: Fungicidal vs Candida, fungistatic vs Aspergillus. Ineffective against Cryptococcus.
  • Anidulafungin ($t_{1/2} \approx 27\text{ h}$): Spontaneous chemical clearance in plasma (safe in hepatic/renal failure).
  • Caspofungin: Used for invasive candidiasis and salvage Aspergillus therapy.
Terbinafine ($t_{1/2} \approx 14\text{ h}$): Concentrates in keratinized tissues (skin, nails). First-line for onychomycosis.

Antiprotozoal Pharmacology & Malaria Hub

Protozoal infections involve intricate life cycles across multiple tissue compartments. Antimalarial drugs are classified by the specific stage of the Plasmodium parasite they eradicate: Tissue Schizontocides (hepatic stage), Blood Schizontocides (symptomatic erythrocytic stage), or Gametocytocides (transmission prevention).

Proguanil & Doxycycline: Act as tissue schizontocides for chemoprophylaxis.

Non-Malarial Protozoal Therapeutics

Amoebiasis (*E. histolytica*)

Rule: Tissue amoebicide (Metronidazole / Tinidazole) MUST be followed by a full course of a luminal amoebicide.

Luminal choice: Diloxanide furoate (or Paromomycin).

Leishmaniasis

First-line: Sodium stibogluconate, Meglumine antimoniate, or Miltefosine (oral).

Severe / Visceral: AmBisome (Liposomal Ampho B).

Toxoplasmosis

Pyrimethamine + Sulfadiazine + Folinic acid.

Pregnancy option: Spiramycin.

Anthelminthic Agents & Infestations

Helminths are multicellular parasitic worms categorized into Cestodes (tapeworms), Nematodes (roundworms), and Trematodes (flukes). Anthelminthics cause worm paralysis (neuromuscular blockade) or disrupt cellular structures (microtubule inhibition).

High-Alert Safety & Toxicity Matrix

Filter anti-infective medications by specific clinical toxicities to review high-risk adverse events, required laboratory monitoring, and genetic screening requirements.

Nephrotoxicity

IV Aciclovir

Crystalline nephropathy caused by drug precipitation in renal tubules during rapid IV administration.

Mitigation: Slow infusion & intravenous hydration.

Nephrotoxicity

Amphotericin B (Deoxycholate)

Distal renal tubular acidosis causing severe hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia.

Mitigation: Switch to lipid formulation (AmBisome); pre-hydrate.

Hepatotoxicity

Nevirapine

Severe, life-threatening hepatitis and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS).

Contraindicated: CD4 >250 (females) or >400 (males).

Bone Marrow Suppression

Zidovudine (AZT) & Ganciclovir

Profound anaemia and neutropenia.

Monitoring: Frequent complete blood count (CBC).

Pregnancy Contraindicated

Efavirenz & Ribavirin

Severe teratogenicity and fetal death risk.

Action: Strict dual-contraception required.
